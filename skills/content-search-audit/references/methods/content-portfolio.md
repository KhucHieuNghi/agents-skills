# Content portfolio and journey coverage

Adapted from Corey Haines' content-strategy audience research, topic grouping,
journey mapping and information-gain review. See [NOTICE](../../NOTICE.md).

## Procedure

1. Map the full declared inventory by content purpose, audience, product/topic,
   language and task stage. Keep unknown classifications explicit. Stages may
   include discovery, evaluation, decision, implementation and support; do not
   force a purchase funnel onto public-service or reference documentation.
2. Match supplied customer questions, search queries, support themes and business
   needs to existing pages. Retain provenance and dated metrics. No keyword
   export means volume and competition are unknown, not model-generated estimates.
3. Build a topic/journey matrix showing served tasks and observed gaps. A missing
   competitor topic is not automatically a customer need. Require demand evidence
   or label the opportunity exploratory.
4. Compare overlapping content for actual task and factual differences. Distinct
   locale, audience, version and product variants can be legitimate. Suggest
   consolidation only with a preservation/redirect/locale review requirement.
5. Inspect navigation between related tasks and the path to the user's next step.
   Identify unreachable useful resources and misleading anchor descriptions.
6. For a proposed new or updated piece, specify the information it would add:
   verified explanation, example, current fact, original evidence or useful tool.
   Do not generate mass pages from query permutations without user value.
7. Prioritize using evidenced user impact, business relevance, confidence,
   dependencies and effort. Preserve these factors separately; do not inherit
   fixed weighted scores, calendar ratios or universal traffic assumptions.

## Output

Task/topic matrix; item-to-question mapping; contradictory claims; duplication
hypotheses; gaps with evidence; and keep/update/investigate/consolidate proposals.
Every proposal names affected item IDs, rationale, verification and uncertainty.
Recommendations to remove/merge content are not executed during audit.

## Limits

Apparent cannibalization requires query/page behavior evidence for confirmation.
A content cluster diagram is not evidence of search demand. Supplied analytics
represent only their collection window and instrumentation. Do not infer expert
consensus or invent customer research to populate the matrix.
