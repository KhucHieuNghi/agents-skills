# Brief and copy

Read the existing brief, brand guidelines, routes, content and legal
requirements before asking the customer to repeat information. Keep the intake
in a local brief or in the handoff if the scope is small. Reuse product
marketing context such as `.agents/product-marketing.md` or
`.claude/product-marketing.md` if present. Distinguish approved facts,
tentative inferences and questions that affect the page.

| Decision                 | Evidence or input to use                                                                       |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Audience and acquisition | Segment, awareness, entry source, priority question and common objection                       |
| Offer and primary action | Deliverable, eligibility, destination, owner and what happens after the click                  |
| Page scope               | New route, approved redesign areas, retained routes or review only                             |
| Identity                 | Approved logo, color and typography roles, asset rights and existing tokens                    |
| Locale                   | Page language, local terminology, region, numbers and formatting                               |
| Proof                    | Approved promises, product facts, testimonials, statistics and permission to publish           |
| Integration              | Existing form or booking flow, test mode, required fields, privacy and measurement constraints |

When information is missing, give the customer specific requests with a reason.
Example: “Please provide the approved demo booking URL so I can verify the CTA
handoff.” A safe preview may use an explicitly labeled local example, omit proof
whose source is unavailable and show a provisional visual treatment. Avoid fake
logos, named customers, reviews, guarantees, metrics, certifications and
functional-looking paths that silently discard submissions.

Ask one batch about decisions the agent cannot resolve. For a minimal
request, ask who visits and from where, what offer they should understand,
and what the primary action should do. Ask about approved brand material,
proof or a reference to use or avoid when it affects the direction. If a
core decision is missing, leave implementation untouched until the
customer answers or explicitly requests a provisional prototype. With a
complete brief, summarize the selected direction and proceed.

Reuse phrases from approved interviews, sales notes or customer feedback
when available. Confirm rights before quoting or showing customer identities.

## Adapted Copywriting pass

- Choose the message that matches the visitor's decision and acquisition
  context. Lead with the job, outcome and audience where needed. Explain the offer
  using concrete product facts. Give each section one visitor question to answer.
- Use one primary action. The label should say what will happen, for example
  “Đặt lịch demo” or “See available dates.” A secondary action must have a clear
  purpose and lower emphasis. Repeated primary links must lead to the same
  intended next step.
- Match the customer's voice and natural phrasing in each language. Translate
  intent and terminology rather than word order. Check headline and CTA wrapping,
  Vietnamese diacritics, and any mobile locale switch.
- In reviews, start from existing copy and change only the agreed areas in
  redesign mode. In new builds, select relevant sections based on visitor needs.
  Add proof, objections, FAQs or details when the material warrants them.
- Record each material claim's exact published wording or location, approved
  source reference and disposition in the handoff. Mark missing evidence and
  leave the claim out of customer-facing copy until confirmed. Keep confidential
  source documents out of public assets and do not collect identifying user data
  in test artifacts.
