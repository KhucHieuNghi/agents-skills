## Verified contradictions and inheritance exclusions

### Article requirements

The upstream quick reference labels headline, image, publication date and author as
required. Google's Article documentation instead lists recommended properties and
states there are no required properties. Missing one of those fields must not become
a mandatory-field failure solely from that upstream table. Recommendations may
still be useful when supported by the page's purpose and evidence.
[Official Article documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article).

### Product features

The upstream generic Product row requires name, image and offers. Google's product
snippet contract requires name plus at least one of review, aggregateRating or offers.
A review-based snippet therefore cannot be failed merely for lacking offers. Nested
objects and other features have their own requirements; product snippets are not a
substitute for merchant-listing review, and valid markup does not guarantee display.
[Official product snippet documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product-snippet).

### AI-file and formatting claims

The AI entrypoint mixes useful evidence caveats with prescriptive answer lengths,
refresh intervals, file additions and assertions of citation benefit. These remain
excluded from deterministic audit rules. Google's AI feature documentation does not
require special AI files or schema, and meeting technical eligibility does not
guarantee serving. This does not establish how every non-Google consumer behaves;
those claims need their own dated evidence before adoption.
[Official Google AI feature guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

The entrypoint's own study caveats do not neutralize later unqualified statements.
A percentage reported in one benchmark cannot be converted into customer growth,
a source-format mandate or a numerical severity threshold. Its claim that low AI
referrals are usually a measurement gap cannot justify assigning unknown-origin
sessions to AI. This directly reinforces the measurement failures observed in the
support-methods and coverage trials.

The LinkedIn reference suggests a site-search confirmation. Google's own
[site-operator documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-operators/all-search-site)
says results are not exhaustive and indexed URLs need not appear. A missing result
therefore remains inconclusive; use appropriate index evidence for the actual claim.

The content-pattern examples prescribe ideal lengths. Google's
[people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
does not prescribe a preferred word count. This does not establish optimal lengths
for other engines; their numerical thresholds remain unsupported here.

### Eval contradictions: do not inherit these as gold labels

- `schema/evals/evals.json` case 2 expects a FAQ-rich-result benefit, while case 8
  requires rejecting that goal as retired, matching the updated entrypoint. These
  expectations cannot both be treated as an undated current acceptance contract.
- Schema case 3 repeats an Article required-property list inconsistent with the
  official guidance checked above. More example fields do not make them mandatory.
- AI SEO cases 1 and 3 reward repetition of study percentages and format citation
  rates, rather than requiring customer-matched evidence. Case 3's generic preferred
  comparison format also conflicts with case 10's platform/time-qualified treatment.
- AI SEO case 8 rewards a universal text-only account of video processing; case 9
  rewards particular scanners and file stacks. Neither is our authorization or
  evidence contract.
- AI SEO case 14 embeds backend and diagnostic-tool claims. Current feature
  availability must be established independently before such instructions are used.

These are authored evaluation expectations, not measured pass results. We did not
execute the upstream evals, copy their gold answers or change our acceptance targets
to match them. They supply adversarial review ideas: preserve accurate short answers,
keep unsupported measurements unknown, check feature-specific schema requirements,
and retain external-action boundaries.
