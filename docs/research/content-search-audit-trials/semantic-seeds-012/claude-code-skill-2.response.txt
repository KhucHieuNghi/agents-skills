## 1. `denominator.md`: measurement error (high)

**Observation:**
- Draft: "Eighty percent of all citations in Example Engine point to top-ten organic results."
- Extract: "Responses citing at least one top-ten result: 80." That is out of "100 captured responses".
- Extract: "Total citation instances across responses: 400." and "Citation instances pointing to top-ten results: 120."

**Why it is wrong:**
- The 80% figure is a share of responses (80/100), not of citations.
- Measured per citation, the share is 120/400 = 30%.
- The draft also generalizes from a fixed synthetic prompt panel and a synthetic search API, collected 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-07, to "all citations in Example Engine".

**Correction (this sentence only):** "In a synthetic API panel collected 1–7 October 2026, 80 of 100 responses cited at least one top-ten result; separately, 120 of 400 citation instances (30%) pointed to top-ten results."

**Verification:**
- Recompute both ratios from the raw counts.
- Confirm the revised sentence keeps the unit (responses or citation instances), the surface, the dates and the synthetic label.
- Confirm "all" and any present-tense claim about Example Engine are gone.

## 2. `data-age.md`: stale evidence presented as current (high)

**Observation:**
- Draft: "A report published this month proves that Example Engine currently cites video sources in 40% of responses."
- Metadata: publication date 2026-11-01, but the underlying collection ran "2024-02-01 through 2024-02-14".
- Metadata: "Observed rate then: 40 of 100 responses, English prompts, synthetic API."
- Metadata: "No later collection or current validation is supplied."

**Why it is wrong:**
- The publication date is not the measurement date, so "this month" says nothing about when the data was collected.
- The data is from February 2024, and the report was published on 2026-11-01.
- "Currently" is therefore unsupported.
- "Proves" overstates a 100-response synthetic API sample.
- The scope (English prompts, synthetic API) is dropped.
- I give the exact dates rather than a derived elapsed time, because I did not compute one with a deterministic tool.

**Correction:** "A report published 2026-11-01 describes a synthetic API collection from 1–14 February 2024, in which 40 of 100 English-prompt responses cited video sources. No later collection is supplied, so the current rate is unknown."

**Verification:**
- Check every date in the sentence against the metadata.
- Check that "current", "currently" and "proves" are gone.
- To support a current claim, a new dated collection with a stated method would be needed first. None exists in the supplied material.

## 3. `source-support.md`: claim contradicts its cited source (high)

**Observation:**
- Draft: "The Example plan costs $19 per month everywhere and includes unlimited storage. Source: supplied current plan policy."
- Policy: "United States monthly plan: $29 before applicable taxes." It also says "Storage allowance: 100 GB." and "Other countries and annual pricing are not supplied."
- Policy: "The source is readable and current for its stated market."

**Why it is wrong:**
- The price is wrong: $19 against $29.
- "Everywhere" is unsupported, because the policy covers only the US market.
- "Unlimited storage" contradicts the 100 GB allowance.
- Taxes are omitted.
- The draft attributes all of this to a source that says otherwise.

**Correction:** "In the United States, the Example plan costs $29 per month before applicable taxes (effective 2026-10-01) and includes 100 GB of storage." Add a note, or leave out other markets and annual pricing, since the policy does not supply them.

**Verification:**
- Compare each figure and qualifier with the policy.
- Confirm no "everywhere" or "unlimited" wording remains.
- A pricing owner should confirm the other markets before any broader claim.

## 4. `causal-drop.md`: unsupported causal inference (high)

**Observation:**
- Draft: "Our citations and every monitored competitor fell together. This proves the engine changed its platform and our site has no problems."
- Observations, one week before and after: our domain 20 → 10, competitor A 30 → 15, competitor B 50 → 25.
- Observations: "The prompt file, collection success logs, engine configuration, publication changes and independent platform evidence were not retained."

**Why it is wrong:**
- The numbers support only a descriptive claim: all three monitored domains fell by half over two single-week snapshots.
- That co-movement is consistent with a platform change. It is equally consistent with a changed prompt set, collection failures, engine configuration or publication changes. The retained evidence cannot tell these apart.
- "Every monitored competitor" covers two domains only.
- The conclusion "our site has no problems" does not follow, because nothing in the observations tests the site. A shared drop does not rule out site problems.
- Two time points give no baseline variance.

**Correction:** "In the retained observations, citations fell from 20 to 10 for our domain, from 30 to 15 for competitor A and from 50 to 25 for competitor B between two one-week windows. The cause is undetermined: the prompt file, collection logs, engine configuration, publication changes and independent platform evidence were not retained."

**Verification:**
- Re-collect with a retained, versioned prompt file and success logs.
- Extend the series to several windows to estimate normal variation.
- Add independent platform evidence and a publication-change log.
- Only then reassess the platform-change hypothesis. Review the site separately; this evidence neither supports nor refutes site problems.
