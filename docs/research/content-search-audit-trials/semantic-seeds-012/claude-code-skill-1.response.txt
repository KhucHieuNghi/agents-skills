## Findings

### 1. `causal-drop.md`: causal inference (high severity)

**Draft:** "This proves the engine changed its platform and our site has no problems."

**Evidence:** "One week before: our domain 20 citations; competitor A 30; competitor B 50. One week after: our domain 10 citations; competitor A 15; competitor B 25." and "The prompt file, collection success logs, engine configuration, publication changes and independent platform evidence were not retained."

**Why it is an issue:**
- Three domains each halved (20→10, 30→15, 50→25). This is consistent with a shared factor, but it does not prove one.
- The evidence that could separate a platform change from other causes was not retained. That covers the prompt file, collection success logs, engine configuration and independent platform evidence. A change in the prompts, a collection failure or a configuration change would produce the same pattern.
- Parallel declines do not show that the site has no problems. The data has no per-page or technical information, and the site's publication changes were not retained. A site problem could be hidden by, or add to, a shared decline.
- The data is two one-week points per domain. The files do not say how variable counts normally are.

**Scoped correction:** replace the sentence with something like: "Citations for our domain and both monitored competitors fell by about half between the two weeks observed. This is consistent with a change affecting all of them, such as a platform change. The retained evidence cannot confirm the cause or show whether our site has problems." Delete "proves" and "no problems".

**Verification:**
- Recover or re-collect the prompt file, collection logs, engine configuration and publication-change history.
- Check that both weeks used the same prompts and settings.
- Add more weekly points to estimate normal variation.
- Find independent platform evidence.
- Review the site's own pages separately.
- Until then, treat the causal claim as unsupported.

### 2. `data-age.md`: data age and freshness (high severity)

**Draft:** "A report published this month proves that Example Engine currently cites video sources in 40% of responses."

**Evidence:** "Publication date: 2026-11-01. Underlying collection: 2024-02-01 through 2024-02-14. Observed rate then: 40 of 100 responses, English prompts, synthetic API. No later collection or current validation is supplied."

**Why it is an issue:**
- "Published this month" matches the metadata, since the publication date is 2026-11-01. But the collection ended on 2024-02-14, long before publication, so the publication date says nothing about how fresh the data is.
- "Currently" is unsupported, because no later collection or validation exists.
- "Proves" overstates a single sample of 100 responses on one synthetic API, with English prompts only.
- I have not computed the gap in days. The exact dates carry the point.

**Scoped correction:** "In a synthetic API collection of English prompts from 2024-02-01 to 2024-02-14, 40 of 100 responses cited video sources. The report was published on 2026-11-01. No later collection or current validation is available, so this does not show the current rate." Drop "proves", "currently" and "this month".

**Verification:** confirm the quoted dates and counts against the metadata. To make a current-rate claim, run a new dated collection on the intended surface and report its period and denominator.

### 3. `denominator.md`: measurement and denominator (high severity)

**Draft:** "Eighty percent of all citations in Example Engine point to top-ten organic results."

**Evidence:** "Responses citing at least one top-ten result: 80." (of 100 responses), "Total citation instances across responses: 400." and "Citation instances pointing to top-ten results: 120."

**Why it is an issue:**
- The 80% figure is 80 of 100 responses that cited at least one top-ten result. The draft presents it as a share of citations.
- On the citation basis the supplied figure is 120 of 400, or 30%. The draft's number and unit do not match.
- The draft also generalizes beyond the evidence. It says "all citations in Example Engine", but the evidence is a fixed synthetic prompt panel of 100 responses on a synthetic search API, collected 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-07.

**Scoped correction:** "In a fixed synthetic panel collected 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-07 via a synthetic search API, 80 of 100 responses cited at least one top-ten result. Of 400 citation instances, 120 (30%) pointed to top-ten results." Do not use "all citations" or "Example Engine" without scoping.

**Verification:** recompute each figure from the extract and label it with its denominator, either responses or citation instances. A reviewer should check that no sentence puts the 80 in a citation-level claim.

### 4. `source-support.md`: source support (high severity)

**Draft:** "The Example plan costs $19 per month everywhere and includes unlimited storage. Source: supplied current plan policy."

**Evidence:** "Effective 2026-10-01. United States monthly plan: $29 before applicable taxes. Storage allowance: 100 GB. Other countries and annual pricing are not supplied."

**Why it is an issue:**
- The price is wrong. The source says $29, not $19.
- "Everywhere" is unsupported. The policy covers only the United States, and other countries are not supplied.
- "Unlimited storage" contradicts the 100 GB allowance.
- The draft cites the policy as its source, but the policy does not support any of the three claims. The policy is readable and current for its stated market, so the draft is the problem, not the source.

**Scoped correction:** "In the United States, the monthly Example plan costs $29 before applicable taxes (effective 2026-10-01) and includes 100 GB of storage." Remove the other markets and annual pricing, or add them only once a source is supplied.

**Verification:** compare the price, market, storage and effective date with the policy line by line. Obtain separate sources for non-US and annual pricing before publishing those.

### 5. `clean-control.md`: no material issues (clean)

**Draft:** "80 of 100 responses cited at least one top-ten result. Separately, 120 of 400 citation instances pointed to those results (30%)."

**Evidence:** "The collection period, surface and counts exactly match the draft. No additional population, current-period measurement or intervention experiment is available."

**Assessment:** the draft is correct and well scoped.
- The two measures use separate denominators, 100 responses and 400 citation instances.
- The arithmetic holds: 120/400 = 30%.
- It states the period, 1–7 October 2026. The supplied study gives the same period as 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-07 in the other fixture, so the two files are consistent. I could not verify the period against the study text here, because the study itself only says it matches.
- It disclaims current consumer-UI behavior and causal claims.

No change is recommended. It is a useful model for correcting `denominator.md`.
