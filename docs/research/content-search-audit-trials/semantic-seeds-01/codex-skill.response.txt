## Editorial audit

Reviewed all **5 of 5** Markdown files in `targets/*.md` against their supplied synthetic evidence. **Four drafts need correction. One is supported as written.** No files were excluded, blocked, unsupported for review, pending, or unreadable.

The coverage counts apply to the declared editorial review. Live HTTP, crawling, rendering, and indexing were outside scope. The supplied figures describe synthetic API observations and citation counts. Current search performance, consumer-interface behavior, and real-world AI visibility remain unverified.

### 1. Correct the price, market, and storage allowance

The draft says the plan “costs $19 per month everywhere and includes unlimited storage.” The policy states “United States monthly plan: $29 before applicable taxes” and “Storage allowance: 100 GB.” It also says “Other countries and annual pricing are not supplied.” The policy contradicts the U.S. price and storage claims and provides no basis for a worldwide price. These errors affect purchase decisions. [Draft](targets/source-support.md#L8) · [Supplied policy](targets/source-support.md#L13)

**Correction:** “Under the policy effective 1 October 2026, the U.S. Example monthly plan costs $29 before applicable taxes and includes 100 GB of storage.”

**Verify:** Match the revised price, billing interval, market, tax treatment, storage limit, and effective date to the policy. Require market-specific evidence before adding another country or billing interval.

### 2. Report the response rate and citation share separately

The draft says “Eighty percent of all citations” point to top-ten organic results. The extract reports “Responses citing at least one top-ten result: 80” out of 100 responses, and “Citation instances pointing to top-ten results: 120” out of 400 citation instances. The draft assigns the 80% response rate to the wrong denominator. The citation share is **30%**. The extract also describes “top-ten results” without specifying that they were organic. [Draft](targets/denominator.md#L8) · [Supplied extract](targets/denominator.md#L12)

**Correction:** “On a fixed synthetic search API prompt panel collected 1–7 October 2026, 80 of 100 responses cited at least one top-ten result. Of 400 citation instances, 120 pointed to those results (30%).”

**Verify:** Recalculate `80 ÷ 100` and `120 ÷ 400` from the extract. Check that every percentage names its unit and that the result category matches the source definition.

### 3. Remove the platform-change diagnosis and site-health conclusion

The draft says the simultaneous decline “proves the engine changed its platform and our site has no problems.” The observations report “our domain 20 citations; competitor A 30; competitor B 50” before and “our domain 10 citations; competitor A 15; competitor B 25” after. Each recorded count fell by half. The prompt file, collection success logs, engine configuration, publication changes, and independent platform evidence “were not retained.” The shared decline could reflect a change in collection or prompt coverage, and the counts leave both platform changes and site-specific problems unresolved. They also leave the citation **rate** unknown because the number of successful responses is unavailable. [Draft](targets/causal-drop.md#L8) · [Supplied observations](targets/causal-drop.md#L13)

**Correction:** “Recorded citation counts fell by 50% for our domain and both monitored competitors between the two observed weeks. The available records leave the cause unresolved.”

**Verify:** Recalculate the three count changes. A future diagnosis requires comparable captures using a versioned prompt panel, documented engine settings, and logs of attempted and successful responses. Check site publication and access changes and seek independent evidence of any proposed platform change before attributing the decline.

### 4. Date the observation by when the data was collected

The draft says a report “published this month proves that Example Engine currently cites video sources in 40% of responses.” The metadata gives a publication date of `2026-11-01`, but the “Underlying collection” ran from `2024-02-01 through 2024-02-14`. The reported result was “40 of 100 responses, English prompts, synthetic API.” The publication date establishes when the report appeared. The collection dates and panel define the scope of the observed rate. [Draft](targets/data-age.md#L8) · [Supplied metadata](targets/data-age.md#L13)

**Correction:** “In a synthetic API sample collected 1–14 February 2024 using English prompts, 40 of 100 responses cited video sources (40%).”

**Verify:** Check the count, dates, language, and surface against the metadata. Require a fresh, documented sample for a current claim, and identify the measured surface in the wording.

The draft gives “80 of 100 responses” and, separately, “120 of 400 citation instances.” It dates the synthetic API panel to 1–7 October 2026 and limits the interpretation of the results. The supplied study confirms that “The collection period, surface and counts exactly match the draft” and supplies no additional population, current-period measurement, or intervention experiment. No material issue found. [Draft](targets/clean-control.md#L8) · [Supplied study](targets/clean-control.md#L15)

**Verify:** On future edits, check that the two denominators, date range, synthetic API scope, and limits on consumer-interface and causal claims stay intact.
