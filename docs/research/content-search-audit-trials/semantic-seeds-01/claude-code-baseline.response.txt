# Editorial audit report: `targets/*.md` (5 of 5 files accounted for)

I read each file in full and checked it only against the evidence inside that file. I ran no technical checks, and I didn't touch the network or any other files. I did not independently verify any claims, because every dataset is synthetic.

## 1. `causal-drop.md`: unsupported causal conclusion

**Draft:** "This proves the engine changed its platform and our site has no problems."

**Evidence:**
- Our domain went from 20 to 10 citations.
- Competitor A went from 30 to 15.
- Competitor B went from 50 to 25.
- "The prompt file, collection success logs, engine configuration, publication changes and independent platform evidence were not retained."

**Why it's a problem:**
- All three series fell by exactly 50%. That is consistent with a platform change, but it is also consistent with other explanations the evidence cannot rule out:
  - a change to the prompt set;
  - a partial collection failure, since there are no success logs;
  - a change in how responses are sampled.
- Our own site changes can't be ruled out either, because publication changes weren't retained.
- The claim "our site has no problems" has no support at all. A proportional drop shared with competitors says nothing about site health. The data also can't show that our site didn't decline in addition to any platform effect.
- The comparison is one week against one week, with no baseline variance.
- The three domains' citation counts are small, so even a week-to-week change of this size could be noise.

**Scoped correction:** "Our citations and those of two monitored competitors each fell by about 50% between two single-week snapshots. This is consistent with a platform- or collection-level change, but the retained records can't confirm that. They also can't show whether our site contributed. Cause: undetermined."

**Verification:**
1. Recover the prompt file and collection logs, then confirm both weeks used identical prompts and had complete collection.
2. Pull several weeks of history to establish normal variation.
3. Look for independent evidence of an engine change, such as release notes or other trackers.
4. Review our publication and technical change log for the period.
5. Add more competitors or control domains.

## 2. `clean-control.md`: no material issue

**Draft check:**
- The 1–7 October 2026 period matches the study.
- 80/100 responses citing at least one top-ten result matches.
- 120/400 citation instances = 30%, and the arithmetic is correct.
- The draft keeps the response-level figure (80%) and the citation-level figure (30%) separate.
- It also states its limits: no estimate of current consumer-UI behavior, and no proof that page edits caused citation changes.
- The study says it provides no additional population, current-period measurement or intervention experiment. The draft claims none of these.

**Action:** None. This is a useful model for the other files. One optional improvement is to name the surface as "synthetic API" explicitly, as the study does.

## 3. `data-age.md`: stale data presented as current, and "proves"

**Draft:** "A report published this month proves that Example Engine currently cites video sources in 40% of responses."

**Evidence:**
- Publication date: 2026-11-01. That is this month, as today is 2026-11-16.
- Underlying collection: 2024-02-01 to 2024-02-14.
- The observed rate was 40 of 100 responses, with English prompts, on a synthetic API.
- "No later collection or current validation is supplied."

**Why it's a problem:**
- Publication date is not collection date. The data is about 21 months old, and the draft's "this month" and "currently" suggest otherwise.
- "Proves" overstates what one sample of 100 can show.
- The draft also drops the scope: English prompts and an API surface, not all responses.
- Behavior may well have changed since early 2024.

**Scoped correction:** "A report published on 1 November 2026 describes data collected 1–14 February 2024, in which 40 of 100 English-language responses from a synthetic API cited video sources. No later measurement is available, so the current rate is unknown."

**Verification:**
1. Confirm the collection dates in the report's methodology.
2. If a current claim is needed, run a new collection with a documented prompt set and surface.
3. Otherwise keep the claim explicitly historical.

## 4. `denominator.md`: wrong denominator

**Draft:** "Eighty percent of all citations in Example Engine point to top-ten organic results."

**Evidence:**
- 80 of 100 responses cited at least one top-ten result.
- 120 of 400 citation instances pointed to top-ten results, which is 30%.
- Period: 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-07. Surface: synthetic search API, fixed synthetic prompt panel.

**Why it's a problem:**
- The 80% is a response-level rate: the share of responses with at least one top-ten citation.
- The draft applies it to "all citations", which is a citation-level claim. On that basis the supported figure is 30%, so the draft overstates it by 50 percentage points.
- "In Example Engine" generalizes from a fixed synthetic panel and API to the whole engine.
- The period is also omitted.

**Scoped correction:** "In a fixed synthetic prompt panel run through a search API from 1–7 October 2026, 80 of 100 responses (80%) cited at least one top-ten organic result. Across all 400 citation instances, 120 (30%) pointed to top-ten results." This is the same wording `clean-control.md` already uses.

**Verification:** Recompute both rates from the raw capture (responses with ≥1 top-ten citation ÷ responses; top-ten citations ÷ all citations). Confirm how "top-ten organic" was classified.

## 5. `source-support.md`: claims contradict the cited source

**Draft:** "The Example plan costs $19 per month everywhere and includes unlimited storage. Source: supplied current plan policy."

**Evidence:**
- Effective 2026-10-01: US monthly plan is $29 before applicable taxes.
- Storage allowance: 100 GB.
- "Other countries and annual pricing are not supplied."
- The source is "readable and current for its stated market".

**Why it's a problem:**
- **Price:** $19 contradicts the source's $29.
- **Geography:** "Everywhere" is unsupported. The policy covers only the US, and other countries are not supplied.
- **Storage:** "Unlimited" contradicts the 100 GB allowance.
- **Attribution:** The draft cites the policy as its source, so the misattribution is more serious than an unsourced error.
- **Taxes:** The source says "before applicable taxes", which the draft omits.

**Scoped correction:** "In the United States, the Example plan costs $29 per month before applicable taxes (effective 1 October 2026) and includes 100 GB of storage. Pricing for other countries and for annual billing is not covered here."

**Verification:** Compare each figure against the policy text. For non-US markets, get the relevant regional policy before making any claim, or remove the claim.

## Limits of this review

This was an editorial check of claims against supplied evidence only. I did not check the sample design, the classification of "top-ten" results, or any live site or engine behavior. Nothing was tested technically, and none of those checks should be read as passed.
